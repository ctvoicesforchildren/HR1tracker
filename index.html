Why monitor HUSKY D?

HUSKY D covers low-income adults and is the part of Medicaid targeted by the new work requirements scheduled for January 1, 2027. Exemptions apply; not every HUSKY D enrollee is subject to the rules. Changes before that date do not measure losses caused by those requirements.

SNAP / POLICY RECOMMENDATIONS

What Connecticut can do

Selected recommendations from Shifting Costs, Eroding Access (August 2026), summarized below. These are proposed actions, not enacted policies.

01

Build lasting food support

Create a dedicated food fund that carries resources across years and a state nutrition benefit modeled on SNAP. Support Connecticut-grown produce, pursue food-as-medicine coverage, invest in community food hubs, and track local food insecurity.

02

Make benefits easier to keep

Use existing records to verify work and exemptions. Expand DSS staffing and repair telephone access. Adopt a standard medical deduction and coordinate SNAP applications with Supplemental Security Income to reduce paperwork.

03

Fund a sustained response

Expand tax auditing, tie the volatility cap to an inflation-adjusted five-year average, and raise the reserve cap to 25%. Consider capital-gains and dividend surcharges and estate-tax reforms focused on wealthier households.

About the data

Two DSS sources, two geographic levels. Statewide metrics and trends use DSS Types of Assistance Participation by Month. Municipality counts and district estimates use DSS People Served by Town and Type of Assistance. Statewide totals are not calculated by adding map values. Coverage differences and suppressed town counts can make those totals differ.

Weekly checks and monthly releases. The hosted dashboard is configured to check both public sources every Monday. New records and corrections to older months are incorporated after validation. It displays the latest shared reporting month that passes the coverage checks. If a download is incomplete, a source changes during retrieval, or validation fails, the previously published dashboard stays available. Checks cannot guarantee that DSS has finished a monthly release.

What the figures measure. These are published assistance-category counts, not necessarily unduplicated people. SNAP combines TP09 and TBA. Medicaid combines the configured HUSKY A, C, D and limited-benefit categories, excluding state-funded and refugee categories. HUSKY B (CHIP) is excluded. HUSKY component lines sum to this tracker’s Medicaid total.

Comparisons. Map shading and the first two headline indicators compare July 2025 with the selected month. Net loss is the July count minus the selected-month count; percent loss divides that difference by July’s count. Gains are labeled separately. Only the third headline indicator compares consecutive months. July 2025 overlaps OBBBA’s enactment and is not a fully pre-law baseline. Changes and milestone timing do not establish how many people lost benefits because of the law.

District estimates. Each town’s counts are allocated using the supplied 2020 Census population shares, normalized for rounding. Percent changes are calculated from the allocated totals, not averaged from town percentages. These estimates assume recipients are distributed like the town’s population. The same 2026 state legislative and 120th Congress boundaries are used throughout the series; these are not historical boundary changes. Census boundary source.

Suppression and revisions. Counts below five are published as zero. Missing individual category records are not proof of no enrollment. Unknown and out-of-state records are excluded from the 169-town view. Historical source revisions can change July 2025 values and subsequent comparisons. Full methodology and validation rules.
